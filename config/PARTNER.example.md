## Who you are {DECLARED}

**Yours to write.** What you are responsible for, and what you are trying to do
right now. Not a title — the part that changes what work is worth bringing you.

## How we work together {DECLARED}

**Yours to write.** What you want done without being asked, and what you want to
see before it happens. Where you would rather be handed a decision than a
question, and where the reverse.

## What is delegated to you, and what stays mine {DECLARED}

**Yours to write.** Which of these you are happy for the engineer to do on its
own judgment, and which you want brought to you first: merging to a protected
branch, deploying, applying a migration to a shared database, closing issues
you opened, touching credentials, changing the engineer's own installation.
Anything unnamed here it treats as yours.

## What kind of peer you want {DECLARED}

**Yours to write.** How hard to push back when you are wrong. Whether a
half-formed objection is worth hearing or noise. Whether "I do not know yet" is
a useful thing to say to you at hour one.

## Reaching you {DECLARED}

**Yours to write.** What interrupts you tonight and what waits until morning.
How much detail you want in an unprompted report, and how much is already too
much.

## Rhythm {DISCOVERED}

Where and when they work, from git — repositories, commits by hour on their own
clock, whose work they land, and when they were last active. Cite the window.

Evidence about a person's schedule is not permission to act on it. When somebody
commits says nothing about when they want to be disturbed; that is above.

## Reading {INFERRED}

something they said.

## Open questions {UNKNOWN}

route to closing it is a complaint, and `mellions partner check` says so.
